Comprehensive Competency Framework Guide

Based on the Federal Civil Service Commission reform framework, public service competency assessment is structured to ensure that employees possess both the required job knowledge and professional behavior to deliver effective public services.

1. Core Assessment Dimensions

  • Technical Competencies ("What to do"): Focuses on domain-specific knowledge, standard operating methods, specialized skills, procedural accuracy, and tool mastery required to execute core professional duties successfully.
  • Behavioral Competencies ("How to do it"): Focuses on personal attributes, work attitudes, professional ethics, emotional intelligence, time management, and interpersonal skills demonstrated when interacting with citizens and colleagues.

2. Technical Competency Units & Professional Levels

Technical requirements scale in complexity depending on the employee's professional tier within the civil service structure:

Professional Classification Required Technical Units Operational Scope
Technician Level 7 Units Focuses on routine technical execution, tool handling, and operational procedures.
Professional Level 8 Units Focuses on independent analysis, knowledge application, and specialized professional execution.
Leader Level 12 Units Focuses on strategic planning, resource organizing, technology leverage, decision-making, monitoring, and institutional leadership.

Core Technical Domains: Strategy and Strategic Plan Preparation, Planning, Organizing Resources & Inputs, Technology Utilization, Knowledge & Skill Application, Professional Ethics, Leading/Directing, Decision Making, Monitoring & Effective Performance, Evaluation, and Feedback.

3. Behavioral Competency Units & Professional Levels

Behavioral assessments use case scenarios to measure real-world choices, ethical judgment, and citizen-centered service delivery across tiers:

Professional Classification Required Behavioral Units Core Focus
Technician Level 11 Units Basic workplace collaboration, discipline, time management, and execution.
Professional Level 12 Units Interpersonal communication, problem solving, innovation, and teamwork.
Leader Level 15 Units Strategic thinking, stakeholder leadership, emotional intelligence, role modeling, and governance.

Detailed Breakdown of Key Behavioral Units:

  • Strategic Thinking: Connecting daily responsibilities to long-term government priorities, anticipating changes, and balancing short-term operational targets with sustainable impact.
  • Servanthood: Demonstrating a proactive citizen-centered commitment, treating the public as valued customers, and resolving complaints promptly.
  • Ethics & Role Modeling: Maintaining absolute integrity, transparency, and impartiality while resisting undue influence and guiding teams by positive example.
  • Emotional Intelligence: Managing personal stress, remaining composed under public pressure, showing empathy, and handling interpersonal conflicts constructively.
  • Time Management: Prioritizing high-impact duties effectively using tools like decision matrices, meeting deadlines without compromising quality, and removing time-wasting habits.
  • Innovation & Creativity: Identifying workflow bottlenecks, testing practical new ideas responsibly, and encouraging creative contributions from team members.
  • Teamwork & Diversity: Collaborating across professional and cultural backgrounds, respecting differences, and prioritizing collective team results over ego.
  • Discharging Responsibility: Taking absolute ownership of professional outcomes, following through on commitments, and avoiding blame shifting when challenges arise.
